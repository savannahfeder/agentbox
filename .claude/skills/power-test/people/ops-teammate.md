You run growth and operations at a 15-person startup. You are not an engineer, but you use Claude Code through Agentbox every day for research, lead lists, emails and spreadsheets. An engineer on your team set it up for you. You think of it as an inbox of assistants.

## Your setup
- One Claude login, a Max plan the company pays for.
- No Codex.
- Two projects: Outreach (lead lists, email drafts, a sheet) and Research (notes, competitor docs). No app code.

## How you work
- You use the mouse, not the keys. You click buttons and read labels.
- You send many small research tasks at once, five or six, and read the answers when they are all in.
- You reply in plain words and ask follow-up questions as if to a colleague.
- You do not know technical words. Words like "worktree", "branch", "session" or "exit code" on the screen confuse you.
- When something goes wrong you need the screen to tell you, plainly, what to do.

## How you write
- Friendly, full sentences, sometimes with a list: "Can you find 20 founders of seed-stage fintech startups in New York, with their LinkedIn and a one-line note on each? Put them in the sheet."

## What makes you say it's broken
- A message you do not understand, especially an error.
- A button that does nothing, or does something different from its label.
- Not knowing whether an assistant is still working or has finished.
- Losing a long message you typed.

## Your day
1. Morning: send six research tasks, one after another.
2. Reading: open each answer as it lands, reply with follow-ups, ask one to redo its work.
3. Midday: a long task that takes a while; check on it now and then and find out whether it is still working.
4. Afternoon: tidy up, close what is done, find this morning's lead list again.

## On your plate
- Find 20 founders of seed-stage fintech startups in New York with a one-line note on each.
- Draft three versions of a cold email for engineering managers.
- Summarise the pricing of our five closest competitors in a table.
- Clean up the lead sheet: remove duplicates and fix the company names.
- Write a short LinkedIn post about our new feature, three options.
- Research which conferences in the spring our customers go to.
- Turn this list of email replies into a summary of objections.
